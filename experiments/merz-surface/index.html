Printing and ageing

New study: ink at the edges - small interruptions and excess ink on the same drawing.

The composition stays fixed. Printing changes coverage across the sheet; the ageing hypothesis removes ink in a few broad regions. These helpers are experiments, not simulations of a historical printing process.

Start by writing the drawing, or open the shared comparison sketch. Choose printAmount, ageAmount, pattern and treatment separately. The pattern is fixed while an amount changes.

Similar ink loss, different distribution

At these fixed settings, light printing lost 2.70% of the clean ink alpha in our browser check; the ageing study lost 2.64%. Compare the spread of the missing ink, rather than just how much is missing. Other seeds and fonts can change these measurements.

The distributions differ, but missing ink alone does not tell us whether a mark was printed that way or changed later. For now, the ageing model stays a study of local ink loss. It is not ready to become a library ageing function.

One surface or separate mark groups

These two ageing studies use amount 0.65 and base seed 17. On the sheet, all marks meet the same field. The other version gives each of four mark groups its own field. Open either sketch to see its measured coverage loss; equal amounts do not guarantee equal total loss.

What remains open

The per-element version uses an independent seed for each mark group. Amounts match, but total coverage loss may differ. Measure that loss before judging spatial relationships. The broad-region model is a proposed way to study ageing. If it reads as another printing defect, we should reconsider it.

Paper discolouration and contour changes are not included. PNG preserves these pixels. This study offers no SVG export. System fonts may differ between computers.
