About p5.merz

When I was in school, we took the bus to Paris, where I saw work by loads of painters and sculptors I already adored. There was one question I held on to until we were on the bus back to Ghent, when I finally asked my teacher:
“Who is KS?”
She smiled and said:
“That is Kurt Schwitters.”

The name p5.merz comes out of that affection for Schwitters' work. His typographical work reached a high point in the magazine Merz (1923–1932). Twenty-one issues were published, numbered 1–24. Issues 10, 22 and 23 were never published.

This library and its language reflect the vocabulary Kurt Schwitters used to document his artistic and typographic work. Words are important, so we kept them.

Type

The three Merz pages this library follows, the covers of numbers 2 and 8/9 and the back of 8/9, were set from a printer's case: a grotesk in several widths and weights, numerals in grotesk or in a didone, found letters, rules and a pointing hand. p5.merz keeps a small case of its own, two variable fonts under the SIL Open Font License: Archivo by Omnibus-Type as the grotesk and Bodoni Moda by Owen Earl as the antiqua, both taken unmodified from the Google Fonts repository. Our thanks to both designers and to the Google Fonts project. The geometric letters of satz() and bauteile() are our own drawings after Van Doesburg, a guest from the same years, kept for constructed letters such as the F in a Merz advertisement.
